# 3 Real-World Bioregional Organizations + Their Data Needs

All three organizations are active, operating at a defined bioregional/watershed scale, with verifiable public presence (websites, IRS filings, agency partnerships, recent activity).

---

## 1. Mattole Restoration Council (MRC)

**Region/bioregion:** Mattole River watershed, Northern California's "Lost Coast" (Humboldt/Mendocino counties). About 300 sq mi, one of the longest undammed rivers in California.

**Mission:** Understand, restore, and conserve the ecosystems of the Mattole watershed (forests, fisheries, soil, and native plant/animal communities) through community-led, landowner-partnered restoration. Established 1983, one of North America's oldest watershed councils.

**Current data tools:** Runs a dedicated "Watershed Information Systems" program built around GIS (geographic information systems) for land cover and watershed condition mapping, handheld GPS and digital field data collection protocols, and a long-running watershed monitoring/adaptive management cycle reviewed by a 20+ member Technical Advisory Committee of agency staff and practitioners. Also offers custom GIS mapping services to partners.

**Data gaps / coordination problems AI or onchain tooling could help with:**
- About 75% of the watershed is privately owned. Data and restoration work is scattered across many individual landowner parcels with no unified real-time dataset. Trust-building and manual coordination substitute for a shared system.
- Field data collection is still largely manual/handheld-device based, with no mention of automated remote-sensing (satellite/drone) pipelines feeding directly into their GIS. An AI-based change-detection layer (erosion, canopy loss, fire fuel buildup) could reduce field-survey burden.
- No visible mechanism for transparently tracking and verifying restoration outcomes (e.g., road decommissioning, streambank stabilization) to funders. An onchain attestation/hypercert-style system could make impact auditable to grantors without added admin overhead.

## 2. Chesapeake Conservancy

**Region/bioregion:** Chesapeake Bay watershed, spanning parts of Maryland, Virginia, Pennsylvania, Delaware, New York, West Virginia, and D.C. (about 64,000 sq mi).

**Mission:** Conserve the lands and rivers of the Chesapeake Bay watershed, connect people to the outdoors, and restore the Bay for future generations, with a specific public goal of conserving 30% of the watershed's lands by 2030.

**Current data tools:** Operates a dedicated Conservation Innovation Center running "Precision Conservation": high-resolution land use/land cover datasets (built for the Chesapeake Bay Program), enhanced flow-path (hydrology) data, and multiple public geospatial web applications (Restoration Prioritization tool, tree canopy analysis, water quality dashboards for partner groups like Arundel Rivers Federation). They also built a citizen water-monitoring app.

**Data gaps / coordination problems AI or onchain tooling could help with:**
- Land cover/land use data is produced and updated periodically (multi-year project cycles) rather than continuously. An AI-driven satellite pipeline could shrink the lag between land-use change and detection across a watershed this large.
- Restoration and water-quality data is generated by many different partner organizations (13+ partners on some grants) using different tools. There is no single shared, verifiable ledger of "who restored what, where, verified how" across the whole multi-state watershed. Onchain attestations could let the Bay Program cross-reference partner-reported outcomes without re-auditing each one.
- Precision Conservation tools are resource-intensive to build and maintain (dedicated technical staff/VP of Technology). Smaller downstream partner orgs likely can't replicate this, creating a data-capability gap between the well-funded regional org and the grassroots groups it serves.

**Sources:**
- https://www.chesapeakeconservancy.org/ (Conservation Innovation Center / Precision Conservation pages)
- https://archive.chesapeakeconservancy.org/

## 3. Klamath Bird Observatory (KBO)

**Region/bioregion:** Klamath-Siskiyou Bioregion, Southern Oregon and Northern California (explicitly self-identified as a "bioregion"), with programming extending across the Pacific Northwest and migratory ranges into Mexico, Central/South America, and the Caribbean.

**Mission:** Advance bird and habitat conservation through science, education, and partnership, using bird communities as ecological indicators to inform land management. Established 2000. Leads the Klamath-Siskiyou Oak Network, a regional habitat coalition.

**Current data tools:** Long-term bird-banding and demographic monitoring network (16+ stations via KBO and its satellite Humboldt Bay Bird Observatory), data shared through the Avian Knowledge Network / Avian Knowledge Northwest node (a regional bird-data-sharing platform), and multi-agency monitoring partnerships with BLM, U.S. Forest Service, and National Park Service across public and private lands.

**Data gaps / coordination problems AI or onchain tooling could help with:**
- Monitoring spans multiple federal land management agencies (BLM, USFS, NPS) plus private landowners, each with separate systems. An interoperable, verifiable data layer could reduce duplicated reporting across agency boundaries.
- Species-indicator data (e.g., at-risk Oregon Vesper Sparrow tracking) is collected to inform land-management decisions, but linking specific monitoring datasets to specific funded restoration actions (like the $6M+ raised through the Klamath-Siskiyou Oak Network) isn't transparently auditable to funders in real time. An onchain funding/impact-tracking layer could tie dollars to verified ecological outcomes.
- AI-assisted bioacoustic/image classification could reduce the labor-intensive manual bird-banding and field ID workload, freeing capacity for the org's small technical team to cover more of the bioregion.
